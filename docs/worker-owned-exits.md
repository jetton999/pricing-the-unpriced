## Takeaways

1. Real estate is usually excluded from worker buyouts. The only Baltimore co-ops owning
   property (Red Emma's, WaterBottle) bought later with separate, larger, layered financing.
2. One lender does most Baltimore deals: BRED (Seed Commons), $20M+ deployed since 2016,
   repayment as a share of net profit, no personal guarantees. NYC analogs: New Economy
   Project, The Working World. DC: LISC, DC Preservation Fund, DHCD.
3. Prices and loan terms are almost never disclosed for worker buyouts. Real-estate deals
   with public money are transparent.
4. Public capital grants fill the gap in every real-estate case. Crowdfunding is real but
   small ($26k–$720k).
5. Seller financing was not reported in any case.
6. The CIT is the only structure that let low-income neighbors, not just tenants or workers,
   buy in and exit.
7. ESOPs are for scale and phase in over years. Co-op conversions of small shops close in
   about six months.
8. Recurring capital stack: senior bank/CDFI debt at 70–75% LTV → subordinated patient loan
   at 2–4% → public grant → community equity raised over time to retire the sub-debt.
