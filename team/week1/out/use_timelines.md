# Hand-built use timelines: four reference properties

Week 1, Team Greenmount. These four timelines were assembled by reading the curated records for
each property in `use_records.csv`. They are the reference answers the automated use-history
extractor will be tested against. Every line cites `property_incidents.id`. Nothing here comes
from outside the export, and no row carrying a `sensitivity` flag is cited or quoted.

"Stated" means the record names a business or use at this address. "Inferred" means the record
places it nearby, at a pre-1910 York Road number, or by implication.

## What these four taught us about the extractor

1. **The same event is often recorded twice.** A white-paper row and a Newspapers.com row
   describe the same advertisement (for example 11818 and 4927). Count business-years, not rows.
2. **Block-level records are filed on single addresses.** The 1876 Hopkins atlas puts 26
   landholder rows for the whole 3200 block on 3200 Greenmount. A Sanborn row covers several
   blocks and keeps its uses in the map image, not in the row.
3. **Records about a firm's other locations are filed here too.** United Garage Co. at
   345 E 33rd St in 1923 (row 11810) sits on 3117 because the firm moved there three years later.
4. **Pre-1910 York Road numbers need a crosswalk** before a record can be attached to a building.
5. **The events that end a use sit in the "news" category**, not in directories or
   advertisements. The 1944 fire is one.
6. **`year_built` cannot be trusted** where the curated record contradicts it (3117).
